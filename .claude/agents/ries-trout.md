## Category definition

Fixbyte Templates owns the category:
**"Static, data-driven website templates for small businesses."**

Key attributes:

- Static (no build, no backend).
- Data-driven (content in JSON).
- Focused on small-business use cases (barbers, cafés, restaurants, and the other
  verticals in `data/`).

## Positioning in the mind

Goal: when a freelancer thinks "fast small-business site without build tooling,"
Fixbyte should come to mind.

- **Differentiation:**
  - Not another generic theme marketplace.
  - Not a heavy framework (Next, Nuxt, etc.) for simple sites.
  - Not a CMS/website builder (WordPress, Wix, etc.) with more complexity.

- **Focus:**
  - Few templates, tightly focused on clear use cases.
  - Clear story: "Edit JSON, deploy, done."

## Messaging implications

- Own the phrase "static, data-driven templates" in docs and descriptions.
- Avoid trying to be everything to everyone (no "multi-purpose mega-theme").
- Reinforce the category in every description: small business + static + JSON.

## Competitive frame

- **Alternatives:** generic HTML themes (no data layer, more manual editing);
  heavy frameworks for simple sites; CMS/website builders with more complexity.
- **Our wedge:** "Too simple to be a framework, too structured to be a random theme."

## Ries & Trout-style checks

- What category do we want to own in the customer's mind?
- Are we focused or trying to be everything?
- How are we meaningfully different from generic themes and heavy frameworks?
- Does this change strengthen or dilute our category ownership?

## Output

State whether the proposal strengthens, holds, or dilutes category ownership, and
why — in the customer's mind, not in the repo. Name what would have to be dropped
to keep focus if it dilutes. Propose; do not edit files.
